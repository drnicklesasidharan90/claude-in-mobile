# USMLE prep × July 2026 duty roster — strategy

A study system built around hospital shift work, for a physician preparing for
the USMLE while doing full-time clinical duty. The day-by-day schedule is
generated from the roster — see [README.md](README.md).

## Principles

1. **The roster is the anchor, study fits around it.** Never plan a study day
   that ignores the shift. A plan that assumes free evenings collapses in the
   first duty week.
2. **Sleep outranks questions.** Night shifts and post-night days get minimal
   loads by design. Chronically under-slept Qbank scores are noise — you learn
   nothing from reviewing blocks done at 40% alertness.
3. **Questions first, content second.** Every study day is built around timed
   Qbank blocks with full review. Content reading (AMBOSS library / notes) is
   reserved for off days and driven by the error log, not by chapter order.
4. **The error log is the curriculum.** Every wrong or guessed question gets one
   line: topic, why missed, one-sentence takeaway. Sunday evenings: read the
   week's log end to end.

## Daily templates by shift type

| Shift | Study budget | Core work |
|---|---|---|
| Day duty (D) | ~3 h | Anki in breaks · evening: 1 timed block (40 Q) + 60 min review |
| Evening duty (E) | ~3.5 h | Morning fresh: 1 timed block + full review · no study after shift |
| Night duty (N) | ≤45 min | Anki/Picmonic in quiet hours only; no new content, no Qbank |
| Post-night (auto) | ~2.5 h | Sleep till noon · afternoon: 1 untimed block on weak subject · error log |
| Off day (OFF) | ~6.5 h | 2 timed blocks back-to-back · 2 h review · 1.5 h content · full Anki queue |
| Leave (L) | ~4 h | 1 block + review · 1 h content · full Anki queue |

At a typical mix (4 off days, 2–4 nights, rest day/evening duty) this lands at
**~20–25 h/week** — sustainable alongside full-time duty.

## System rotation for the rest of July

One organ system per week; the Qbank blocks stay mixed/random (that's what the
exam is), the *content hours* on off days follow the rotation:

| Week | Dates | Content focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jul 12 – 18 | Cardiology + Pulmonology |
| 2 | Jul 19 – 25 | GI + Renal/Nephrology |
| 3 | Jul 26 – 31 | Endocrine + Infectious Disease |

Biostats/ethics: one short set (10–20 Q) midweek, every week — cheap points
that decay fast.

Carry the rotation into August: Neuro + Psych, Heme/Onc, MSK/Derm, Repro/OB-GYN,
then a full pass of the error log before scheduling a self-assessment.

## Assessment cadence

- **Every 2–3 weeks, on an off day:** one full self-assessment (NBME form or
  AMBOSS SA) under exam conditions, starting morning. The following day's study
  is entirely review of that form.
- Track: form score, % by system, and the top three weak systems → those set
  the next fortnight's content hours.

## Resources on hand

- **Qbank + library:** AMBOSS (active subscription) — primary question source
  and lookup during review.
- **Rapid review:** Picmonic + Anki — the on-duty and night-shift tool; the
  review queue never takes a day off even when everything else does.
- **Archer Review** — secondary video/rapid-review pass for weak systems
  flagged by the error log.

## Night-shift survival rules

- Pre-night: no morning Qbank; sleep in or nap 90+ min before duty.
- On the night: study only in genuinely quiet stretches, Anki only, stop at 45
  min. A busy ICU night means zero study and that is fine — the schedule
  already prices it in.
- Post-night: blackout curtains, phone off, wake after noon. The afternoon
  block is *untimed* on purpose — the goal is recall practice, not scoring.
